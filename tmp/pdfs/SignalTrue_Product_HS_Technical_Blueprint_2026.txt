1. Executive summary
SignalTrue should not be positioned primarily as a burnout-prediction product or as a generic wellbeing 
dashboard. The stronger, more defensible and more urgent product is a continuous work-pattern risk and 
evidence system. It helps Health & Safety, People & Safety, Operations and executives see persistent 
workload exposure, capacity depletion, workday expansion, fragmentation and failed recovery before the 
first reliable signal becomes a long absence, a claim, turnover or delivery failure.
The commercial wedge is especially strong in markets where employers have explicit duties to manage 
psychosocial hazards and fatigue. In Australia, the model WHS framework requires psychosocial risks to be 
eliminated or minimised so far as is reasonably practicable. NSW has strengthened psychosocial-risk 
requirements and now explicitly applies the hierarchy of controls. NSW also approved a Managing the Risk 
of Fatigue at Work Code of Practice in February 2026. [R1-R4]
The product should therefore close an evidence loop rather than merely display analytics: observe work 
patterns, detect sustained exposure, require human review, support worker consultation, document 
controls, measure control effectiveness, trigger re-review, and retain the evidence history.

2. Product direction and why it matters
2.1 The old center of gravity
Earlier SignalTrue concepts emphasized burnout, disengagement, a Team Energy Index and broad 
behavioral early warning. Those ideas created a compelling story but mixed direct observation with 
psychological interpretation. That is a scientific and commercial weakness. Metadata can show work-pattern 
change. It cannot establish that a person is burned out, fatigued, disengaged, unmotivated or about to resign.
2.2 The new center of gravity
The new center is the work system. SignalTrue observes how demand is distributed through calendars and 
collaboration metadata, whether pressure persists, whether people are compensating outside normal work 
boundaries, whether usable capacity is shrinking, and whether a team returns toward its own prior 
operating range after pressure ends.

3. The problems customers actually pay to solve
3.1 Long-duration absence discovered too late
The expensive event is not the first day of sick leave. The expensive event is the period before it, when 
demand may already be persistently high and management still has a chance to redesign workload, remove 
coordination, add capacity or change timing. Safe Work Australia reports that serious mental-health-
condition claims in 2023-24p had a median 35.7 weeks of time lost and median compensation paid of AUD 
67,400. [R5] SignalTrue must not claim it prevents such claims. The product value is earlier visibility into 
work-pattern exposure that can justify earlier review and action.
Customer job
Show me which teams are maintaining delivery by consuming reserve capacity or expanding work 
beyond normal boundaries, before absence or failure becomes the first reliable signal.
3.2 Hidden capacity risk
A team can hit every SLA while its reserve disappears. Conventional KPIs remain green because people 
compensate. SignalTrue should expose the difference between healthy delivery and compensated delivery. 
The output is not a vague workload score. It is evidence that usable focus windows have shrunk, after-hours 
activity has risen, calendar occupancy is elevated and the pattern has persisted.
3.3 Legal and governance evidence gaps
An H&S professional may have an annual risk assessment, a pulse survey, incident reports and EAP data, 
but still lack continuous evidence of how high job demands develop between those snapshots. SignalTrue 
can fill part of this gap by measuring work-pattern exposure. It should never claim that digital metadata 
alone completes a psychosocial risk assessment. Worker consultation and contextual review remain 
necessary.
3.4 Controls that exist on paper but are not verified
A company may introduce a no-meeting day, staffing change, meeting reduction, workload redistribution or 
manager rule and then wait months for another survey. SignalTrue can compare pre-control and post-
control work patterns and identify improvement, no material change, worsening or displacement. This is a 
stronger buying reason than another dashboard because it tells the H&S owner whether the intervention 
changed the measured exposure.
3.5 Gradual deterioration that becomes normal
Slow changes are easy to normalize. A few extra meetings, a small increase in evening activity and a small 
loss of focus time can become the new normal without a single dramatic week. SignalTrue needs long-term 
baselines and persistence logic so the model does not simply relearn dysfunction as normal.

3.6 Business continuity in critical teams
Where a critical team has no reserve, one resignation, incident, major customer or unexpected project can 
push the function into failure. Capacity risk is therefore not only a wellbeing problem. It is an operational-
continuity problem. This is the bridge from H&S budget to COO and CFO value.
4. Health & Safety opportunity and regulatory fit
4.1 The H&S job to be done
For a Health & Safety buyer, the product should map to the work they are already accountable for: identify a 
hazard, understand exposure, review context with workers, choose controls, assign ownership, monitor 
effectiveness, review when conditions change, and retain evidence.
4.2 Australia and NSW fit
Safe Work Australia's model Code of Practice on managing psychosocial hazards states that PCBUs must 
eliminate or minimise psychosocial risks so far as is reasonably practicable. The model Code provides 
practical guidance for identifying, assessing, controlling and reviewing psychosocial hazards. Its legal effect 
depends on adoption in each jurisdiction. [R1]
SafeWork NSW states that the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2025 strengthened the requirements for 
managing psychosocial risks. Section 55C explicitly requires use of the hierarchy of controls where 
elimination is not reasonably practicable, with relevant matters under section 55D considered when 
controlling psychosocial risks. SafeWork NSW emphasizes higher-order controls such as redesigning work 
before relying on lower-order controls such as coping strategies. [R2-R3]
SafeWork NSW lists role overload, high workloads or job demands as a psychosocial hazard. [R6] NSW also 
approved a fatigue Code of Practice on 20 February 2026. The broader Safe Work Australia fatigue model 
Code emphasizes work hours, breaks, work design and consultation. [R4, R7] This makes digital work-
boundary and scheduling exposure a particularly relevant SignalTrue module, provided the product uses 
exposure language and does not claim to diagnose fatigue.

5. Scientific and organizational research foundation
5.1 Why digital trace data is relevant
Kalliomäki-Levanto and colleagues used 90 weeks of timestamped digital trace data to analyze dynamic job-
demand processes. They argue that trace data can provide action-oriented, context-specific and time-
sensitive information that periodic surveys cannot provide in the same way. [S1] This is one of the strongest 
direct methodological supports for SignalTrue's approach.
Zacher and Rudolph's 33-wave longitudinal research on workplace digitalization and workload also 
reinforces a critical warning: relationships can be reciprocal. More digital activity can accompany more 
workload, but a changing workload can also produce more digital activity. SignalTrue therefore must avoid 
causal language unless an intervention or study design supports it. [S2]
5.2 Demand, resource and recovery models
The Job Demands-Resources model distinguishes demands from resources and shows why workload alone is 
incomplete. Demands are strongly related to exhaustion, while a lack of resources is especially related to 
disengagement. [S3] SignalTrue can observe some demands and some structural resources, such as available 
time and schedule predictability. It cannot infer autonomy, fairness, recognition or meaning from metadata.
McEwen's allostatic-load work makes persistence and failure to shut down more important than a single 
peak. [S4] This supports the product distinction between temporary intensity and sustained activation.

Recovery research by Sonnentag and Fritz similarly reinforces the need to distinguish opportunity for 
recovery from actual recovery experience. [S5]
5.3 Interruption and meeting architecture
Gloria Mark and colleagues showed that interrupted work can be compensated for by working faster while 
increasing stress and frustration. [S6] Meeting-to-work transition research also supports the practical 
importance of transition and recovery around meetings. [S7] The product implication is that calendar 
whitespace is not automatically usable focus time. SignalTrue should calculate usable focus windows, 
meeting islands, transition deficits and back-to-back chains.
5.4 Organizational drift, routines and adaptation
Cyert and March's behavioral theory of the firm, March's exploration/exploitation work, Rasmussen's 
dynamic risk model, Dekker's drift-into-failure work and resilience engineering all point in the same 
direction: organizations adapt locally and gradually, often through routines and workarounds. SignalTrue 
can observe part of that adaptation through changes in recurring meetings, coordination load, work 
boundaries and recovery patterns. These theories do not validate a SignalTrue score, but they provide a 
strong conceptual basis for longitudinal drift and recovery analysis. [S8-S13]
5.5 Organizational networks as a secondary engine
Large-scale organizational-network research shows that communication metadata can reveal network 
centralization, silos, cross-boundary ties and structural dependence. Remote-work research at Microsoft 
found meaningful changes in collaboration networks when work arrangements changed. [S14-S18] These 
capabilities are valuable, but they should be used as secondary diagnostics until the H&S exposure and 
control loop is commercially proven.
5.6 Monitoring and privacy warning
Electronic monitoring can itself affect employee experience. Research on workplace monitoring and 
wellbeing sensing highlights trust, purpose, governance and interpretation risks. [S19-S21] SignalTrue 
therefore needs explicit anti-weaponization rules, cohort-level reporting, visible methodology, data 
coverage, purpose limitation and no individual psychological scoring.

6.2 Prohibited claims and uses
 No probability that an individual will burn out, become fatigued, resign, fail or disengage.
 No diagnosis of mental-health, medical or psychological conditions.
 No individual employee productivity ranking.
 No employment, compensation, promotion, disciplinary or termination decisions from SignalTrue 
scores.
 No causal statement such as “meetings caused burnout” from observational metadata alone.
 No claim that a model band is an industry norm unless externally validated.
 No content analysis if the product promise remains metadata-only. If a provider API returns content, 
strip it before persistence and do not send it to an LLM.
6.3 Required context on every signal
Every model-generated signal should show data coverage, comparison period, baseline type, affected cohort 
size, the observed metrics underneath the model, and a short statement of what the signal does not prove. 
This is not UI clutter. It is part of the evidence contract.

7.2 Capacity Pressure
Purpose: detect when a team is losing its ability to absorb additional demand. This is more commercially 
useful than a generic “workload score.” It combines calendar occupancy, usable focus windows, meeting 
density, workday expansion, after-hours activity and optionally schedule volatility. It should be described as 
capacity pressure or reserve depletion, not employee energy.
7.3 Drift and Persistence
Purpose: identify gradual structural movement that humans normalize. SignalTrue should compare current 
behavior with both recent and long-term references. Drift should trigger only when direction, persistence 
and data quality agree. A 30-day moving baseline alone is unsafe because it can eventually normalize a 
deteriorated state.

7.4 Recovery and Return-to-Range
Purpose: separate a normal surge from a persistent work pattern. SignalTrue should measure how quickly 
indicators return toward their pre-event or historical range after an event or control. It can measure 
recovery of the work pattern, not biological or psychological recovery.
7.5 Digital Work & Fatigue Exposure
Purpose: provide objective work-pattern evidence relevant to fatigue review. Candidate indicators include 
extended digital workday, repeated after-hours activity, weekend activity, short interval between last and 
next digital work event, days with insufficient scheduled breaks, high back-to-back density and structural 
timezone compensation. Thresholds should be configurable to company policy and jurisdiction. They are 
exposure indicators, not a fatigue diagnosis.
7.6 Control Effectiveness
Purpose: answer “Did the control reduce the measured exposure?” The engine compares a defined pre-
control window, guard period and post-control window across target and spillover metrics. It must be able 
to identify displacement, where a no-meeting Friday reduces Friday meetings but pushes them into 
Thursday evening or Monday morning.

8.2 Exposure episode logic
An episode should be created only when a configured indicator or multi-signal pattern meets minimum data 
coverage and persistence conditions. Avoid one-off red alerts. A practical MVP rule is: deviation above a 
team-specific threshold on at least three eligible days in a week, repeated across at least two of three 
consecutive weeks, with sufficient coverage. Exact bands remain product rules until validated.
8.3 Exposure priority score
Use a transparent prioritization formula rather than a “risk probability”:
Recommended formula
Priority = 0.35 × intensity + 0.25 × persistence + 0.20 × frequency + 0.10 × cross-signal agreement + 
0.10 × data confidence. All inputs should be normalized and visible. The exact weights are internal 
until validated, and customers should always see the underlying observed metrics.

9. H&S risk workflow and control loop
Figure 3. End-to-end H&S workflow from observation to evidence.
9.1 Signal to risk case
1. SignalTrue detects a persistent work-pattern exposure episode at team level.
2. The H&S owner reviews underlying metrics, data coverage, affected cohort size and known business 
events.
3. The owner chooses “Open review,” “Expected temporary peak,” “Monitor,” or “Dismiss with reason.”
4. If review is opened, SignalTrue creates a Risk Case linked to the exposure episode.
5. The H&S owner records worker consultation or launches a short anonymous pulse.
6. The owner chooses or records controls, assigns an accountable owner, due date, target metrics and 
review date.
7. SignalTrue creates the pre-control baseline and begins post-control monitoring.

9.3 Consultation pulse
Keep the pulse short and contextual. The product should not attempt to become another engagement survey 
platform. Example after a high-job-demand signal: “Is the current workload sustainable within normal 
working hours?”, “Can you take adequate breaks and disconnect after work?”, and “What is creating the 
greatest workload pressure?” Use anonymous team-level reporting and a configurable minimum response 
threshold.

10.3 UX principles
 Action first. Default view is a prioritized work queue, not a wall of scores.
 Observed evidence first. Model index second. AI explanation last.
 Trend and persistence over point-in-time color.
 Always show why a signal exists and what evidence it is based on.
 Always show “what this does not mean” for sensitive outcomes.
 Use neutral review language: “Needs review,” “Persistent exposure,” “Insufficient data,” not “employee 
at risk.”
 Every intervention has an owner, review date and target measure.
 Every report should be understandable without opening the dashboard.
 No employee leaderboard, no individual psychological state, no manager productivity score.

should show team, exposure type, duration, direction, confidence and next action. Avoid displaying a 0-100 
risk number as the main object.
11.4 Exposure detail
The detail page should contain: plain-language finding; timeline; observed metrics; frequency, duration and 
intensity; baseline type; data coverage; known events; related risk cases; consultation; recommended review 
questions; and an explicit limitation statement.
Example finding
Finance Operations has shown persistent high job-demand exposure for 8 weeks. After-hours 
activity and meeting occupancy remain above the team’s historical range while usable focus 
capacity has declined. This pattern does not establish fatigue or burnout. Review workload, staffing, 
deadlines and worker feedback.
11.5 Risks & Controls
This is the operational heart of the H&S product. Default view is a case table: hazard, cohort, owner, control, 
review date, status and effectiveness. A Kanban view is optional but should not replace the table because 
H&S users often need sortable evidence and export.
11.6 Control detail
Example control
Control: remove two recurring reporting meetings and redistribute one customer portfolio. Owner: 
Head of Customer Success. Target: reduce weekly meeting participant-hours by 15% and after-hours 
share by 20% without increasing Monday/Thursday spillover. Review: 30 days.
11.7 Control-effectiveness review
Show pre-control window, post-control window, target metrics and spillover metrics in one comparison. The 
primary answer should be categorical and explainable: Effective, Partially effective, No material change, 
Displaced, Worsened or Insufficient data.

“after-hours activity” and “scheduled break opportunity.” Do not label employees or teams as fatigued. 
Include jurisdiction/policy configuration and data limitations.

17. Privacy, security and AI guardrails
17.1 Privacy architecture
 Default reporting at team/cohort level only.
 Target privacy floor: 8 people for H&S analytics, with customer-specific policy configuration only after 
privacy review.
 No individual-level H&S risk scores in manager or executive views.
 Pseudonymize event identities before analytical storage where technically feasible.
 Strip message body, subject, attachment content and sensitive free text unless a separate, explicitly 
consented product scope exists. The current strategic promise should remain metadata-only.
 Use the shortest raw-event retention that supports recalculation and audit; aggregate longer only where 
justified.
 Expose data-source coverage and suppression decisions to customers.
 Separate system-administration permissions from sensitive H&S case access.
 Log all privileged access and exports.
17.2 AI role
AI should never calculate the authoritative exposure state. Deterministic code should calculate observed 
metrics, baselines, episodes, control deltas and status. The LLM receives only structured aggregated facts 
plus approved context and can produce: explanation, questions to investigate, a draft review note, suggested 
reversible controls and report narrative.
AI guardrail
The AI may say “After-hours activity rose 31% and persisted for 7 weeks.” It may not say “The team 
is burned out” or “The manager caused the overload.”

18.2 Pilot study design
Start with 5-10 organizations where H&S owners will label exposure episodes and record real controls.

aggregate outcome. This can eventually support organization-specific evidence and better priors without 
turning the product into individual surveillance.

Closing product decision
The valuable addition to SignalTrue is not another score. It is the conversion of existing metadata and drift 
capability into a complete work-pattern risk loop: exposure -> review -> consultation -> control -> measured 
effect -> evidence. This is the part that makes the product easier for a Health & Safety buyer to justify, gives 
operations a direct capacity story, and creates a stronger scientific boundary than individual burnout 
prediction.
North-star product question
Where is the way work is currently organized becoming persistent, expensive or unsafe enough to 
require review, and did the organization’s action actually improve it?
